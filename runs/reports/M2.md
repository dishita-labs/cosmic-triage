## Decisions made (non-obvious, unilateral)

1. **Gate slot `always` for the M2 batch.** The entropy gate needs the calibrated M3 belief; on the uncalibrated Fink stub its threshold would be arbitrary. `EntropyGate` is implemented, tested and selectable by config.
2. **Stub belief from Fink scores** (`belief/stub.py`, §10.4) — uncalibrated, used only to drive the gate and printed as "stub"; never a metric.
3. **Jev and Claude never gate acceptance in M2**: the deterministic verifier decides; Jev scores are logged alongside (the M4 comparison input).
4. **Discovery reports produce no claim** (no claim kind exists); earlier TNS discoveries at the position become `prior_flares` (basis `tns_discovery`).
5. **`prior_flares` payload generalised** (investigator-local payload model, not a contract): `earlier_activity_mjd`, `first_known_detection_mjd`, `gap_days`, `basis ∈ {alert_hist_start, tns_discovery}`, `ref`.
6. **SIMBAD as-of rule**: transient types/designations dropped; redshift kept only if its bibcode year is strictly before t's year (dated to Jan 1 of that year).
7. **Sherlock treated as static** (catalogue redshifts assumed to predate t). Caveat for M4 Tier-A.
8. **Lasair batched prefetch** to stay within 100 calls/h.
9. **Factory takes ready-built clients** so `investigator` never touches the cache (import-linter rule kept rather than routed around).
10. **Labels never enter traces**: selection strata live in `runs/exploratory/m2/selection.json`.
11. **Model prices in `budget.yaml` are assumptions** (Sonnet $3/$15 per MTok, Jev $0.042 input) — to be checked before approval; an unknown model id is refused.

## Learning checkpoint 2 (for the owner)

- *Why orchestration is code-owned with swappable slots:* budgets, stops and ordering are enforced by code, so a model can never run unbounded, and each slot (gate, extractor, verifier) can be swapped for its baseline to measure its value.
- *Why claims are typed and validated before they can matter:* the only bridge from text to a decision is a `Claim` that survived schema, provenance, date ≤ t, recomputed geometry and type checks; E1 shows why — a plausible-looking "z = 0.0" is a placeholder, and only typed validation catches it systematically.
- *Why Jev is a gate/verifier, not a classifier:* its strengths are bounded yes/no judgements over pre-structured state; classification needs calibrated probabilities from evidence channels.
- *The path of one claim:* `simbad_cone` (as-of: bibcode 2020 < 2025) → evidence `ev-simbad-…` in the ledger → `RuleExtractor` drafts `host_redshift` → validator: schema ✓, provenance ✓, date ≤ t ✓, geometry recomputed ✓, z ∈ (0, 3] ✓, support ✓ → accepted → `ClaimEffects` (M3: absolute-magnitude constraint). It can be rejected at every one of those checks, and before that at the tool's as-of filter or the registry's.
